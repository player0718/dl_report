\paragraph{Reference run.}
We evaluate the baseline method defined in \cref{sec:method:baseline}
under the shared training and evaluation protocol of
\cref{sec:exp:training,sec:exp:eval}. We consider two training budgets,
30 epochs and 100 epochs, in order to examine both the short-budget
performance and the effect of extended training. These runs serve as the
common reference points for all later comparisons.

\paragraph{Headline numbers.}
Under the 30-epoch setting, the baseline reaches its best validation
accuracy of \textbf{82.78\%} at epoch \textbf{22}, with a training loss
of \textbf{0.4877} and a validation loss of \textbf{0.6695}. The
checkpoint selected at this epoch achieves a test accuracy of
\textbf{82.95\%}. Under the 100-epoch setting, the best validation
accuracy is \textbf{80.42\%}, obtained at epoch \textbf{74}, with a
training loss of \textbf{0.3236} and a validation loss of
\textbf{0.9317}. The corresponding test accuracy is \textbf{79.65\%}.
These results are used as the reference values in the later method
comparisons.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The 30-epoch run gives stronger validation and test performance than the
100-epoch run, despite the latter achieving a substantially lower
training loss. This indicates that simply extending the training budget
does not improve generalization for the baseline model. In the 100-epoch
setting, after the best checkpoint at epoch 74, the validation accuracy
shows small fluctuations and a slight downward trend, while the training
performance continues to improve. Together with the higher validation
loss, this suggests mild overfitting in the later stage of training. The
baseline is therefore a suitable reference point: it provides competitive
short-budget performance, while still leaving room for later methods to
improve robustness and generalization under longer training.
