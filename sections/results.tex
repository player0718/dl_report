\subsection{LLRD + Staged Fine-tuning Results}
\label{sec:llrd}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/llrd_staged_finetuning.png}
  \caption{LLRD with staged fine-tuning: training curves and learning-rate schedule.}
  \label{fig:llrd-staged-curves}
\end{figure}

We next evaluate the proposed optimization strategy based on layer-wise
learning rate decay (LLRD) and staged fine-tuning. Under the same
backbone, dataset, and 100-epoch training budget as the baseline, this
method achieves a best validation accuracy of \textbf{86.06\%} at epoch
\textbf{22}, with a corresponding test accuracy of \textbf{85.33\%}.
Compared with the 100-epoch baseline validation accuracy of
\textbf{80.42\%}, this gives an improvement of \textbf{5.64} percentage
points. The test accuracy also improves from \textbf{79.65\%} to
\textbf{85.33\%}, corresponding to a gain of \textbf{5.68} percentage
points.

As shown in \cref{fig:llrd-staged-curves}, the training curves indicate
that the method improves rapidly after the model is switched from
head-only training to full-model fine-tuning. Validation accuracy rises
sharply in the early part of Stage 2 and reaches its best result around
epoch 22. After that point, training accuracy continues to increase,
while validation accuracy saturates and fluctuates slightly, suggesting
the onset of mild overfitting. Meanwhile, the validation loss remains
relatively stable after the stage switch, which indicates that the
staged schedule avoids severe instability during full-model fine-tuning.
Overall, these results show that LLRD with staged fine-tuning provides a
substantial improvement over the 100-epoch baseline and is an effective
way to adapt a pretrained ViT to CIFAR-100.
