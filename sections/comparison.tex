\subsection{Weight Decay Analysis}
\label{sec:comp:wd-analysis}
With the updated 100-epoch horizon, weight decay behaves as a weak but
measurable regularization knob. The no-decay control reaches $81.81\%$
test accuracy, while $\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$ reaches the best validation
accuracy ($82.56\%$) and the best test accuracy ($82.23\%$). The absolute
gain is modest ($+0.42$ percentage points), and the neighboring nonzero
settings $\lambda=10^{-4}$ and $\lambda=10^{-2}$ obtain similar test
accuracy ($82.21\%$ and $82.17\%$). This pattern suggests a broad,
low-amplitude optimum rather than a sharply tuned coefficient.

\Cref{fig:wd-best-val,fig:wd-gap} also shows the cost of making the
regularizer too strong. The aggressive setting $\lambda=10^{-1}$ drops
to $81.25\%$ test accuracy, below the no-decay control. Its lower
training accuracy indicates capacity suppression rather than useful
regularization. Compared with the original 30-epoch sweep, the 100-epoch
run exhibits a much larger train--val gap (about 11 percentage points
instead of about 5), so moderate decay becomes more useful only after the
longer schedule creates more overfitting pressure. Even then, the effect
size remains small enough that the ranking should be treated cautiously
without multi-seed confirmation.

\subsection{Dropout Analysis}
Overall, dropout-based regularization is effective; compared to the baseline (79.65\%), all methods improved test performance, with DropPath showing the most significant improvement while also significantly reducing the gap, demonstrating stronger generalization ability. The advantage of DropPath stems from the structural characteristics of ViT. Unlike CNNs, ViT layers have more uniform representations, and features are primarily propagated through residual connections rather than by constructing new representations layer by layer. Previous studies have shown that skip connections in ViTs play a dominant role in information propagation, making the model behave more like an iterative optimization of the same representation~\cite{raghu2021vision}. In this context, standard Dropout only applies perturbations to local features, whereas DropPath acts directly on the residual paths, performing regularization at the level of information flow. By randomly dropping entire sub-layers, DropPath alters the effective depth and feature propagation paths, thereby more effectively mitigating overfitting.

From the perspective of parameter selection, moderate regularization is more reasonable. Standard Dropout performs best at a rate of 0.3, while higher rates do not yield further improvements. In contrast, Attention Dropout and Patch Dropout have limited impact: the former is insensitive to local perturbations due to the global modeling capability of self-attention; the latter is robust to the loss of a small number of patches because of ViT’s global aggregation. Therefore, these two methods struggle to significantly improve generalization performance.
