\subsection{Weight Decay Analysis}
\label{sec:comp:wd-analysis}
Within the shared 30-epoch fine-tuning protocol, weight decay behaves as
a weak regularization knob rather than a dominant driver of
generalization. The best test accuracy remains the no-decay baseline
($84.75\%$ at $\lambda=0$), while the closest nonzero setting,
$\lambda=10^{-2}$, reaches $84.74\%$; this difference is too small to
support a meaningful practical advantage for tuning weight decay in this
regime. At the same time, the strongest decay in the sweep
($\lambda=10^{-1}$) attains the highest validation accuracy
($84.56\%$) and the smallest train--val gap ($4.97$ percentage points),
showing that validation-selected and test-selected optima do not fully
coincide.

\Cref{fig:wd-best-val,fig:wd-gap} suggests that the main effect of
stronger decay is a modest reduction in memorization rather than a large
improvement in final accuracy. Relative to the baseline, $\lambda=10^{-1}$
reduces the gap at the best-validation epoch by about $0.41$ percentage
points while preserving competitive validation performance. However,
$\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$ lowers both validation and test accuracy
without reducing the gap, which is more consistent with capacity
suppression than with useful regularization. Taken together, the sweep
indicates that pretrained ViT-Tiny on CIFAR-100 is only mildly
overfitting under this short training horizon, leaving limited room for
weight decay to improve upon the baseline.

        dropout (\cref{sec:dropout}).
  \item \textbf{Output-level}: label smoothing (\cref{sec:lsearly}).
  \item \textbf{Training-process-level}: early stopping
        (\cref{sec:lsearly}).
