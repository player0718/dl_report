\subsection{Weight Decay Analysis}
\label{sec:comp:wd-analysis}
With the updated 100-epoch horizon, weight decay behaves as a weak but
measurable regularization knob. The no-decay control reaches $81.81\%$
test accuracy, while $\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$ reaches the best validation
accuracy ($82.56\%$) and the best test accuracy ($82.23\%$). The absolute
gain is modest ($+0.42$ percentage points), and the neighboring nonzero
settings $\lambda=10^{-4}$ and $\lambda=10^{-2}$ obtain similar test
accuracy ($82.21\%$ and $82.17\%$). This pattern suggests a broad,
low-amplitude optimum rather than a sharply tuned coefficient.

\Cref{fig:wd-best-val,fig:wd-gap} also shows the cost of making the
regularizer too strong. The aggressive setting $\lambda=10^{-1}$ drops
to $81.25\%$ test accuracy, below the no-decay control. Its lower
training accuracy indicates capacity suppression rather than useful
regularization. Compared with the original 30-epoch sweep, the 100-epoch
run exhibits a much larger train--val gap (about 11 percentage points
instead of about 5), so moderate decay becomes more useful only after the
longer schedule creates more overfitting pressure. Even then, the effect
size remains small enough that the ranking should be treated cautiously
without multi-seed confirmation.

model can be constrained:

        dropout (\cref{sec:dropout}).
  \item \textbf{Output-level}: label smoothing (\cref{sec:lsearly}).
  \item \textbf{Training-process-level}: early stopping
        (\cref{sec:lsearly}).
