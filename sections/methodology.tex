\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

This report is organized around a controlled comparison of five
regularization families for ViT-Tiny/16 on CIFAR-100. The
regularization family is the primary independent variable; the model,
data split, optimization budget, and evaluation metrics are held fixed
and are described later in \cref{sec:experiments}. This section defines
the baseline and the method-specific design choices that the later
experiments evaluate.

\subsection{Baseline}
\label{sec:method:baseline}
Our reference system uses ViT-Tiny/16 fine-tuned on CIFAR-100 with no
explicit regularization beyond the minimal preprocessing required by the
model. Concretely, weight decay is set to $0$, dropout is disabled, no
label smoothing is used, early stopping is disabled, and the input
pipeline is limited to resize plus ImageNet normalization. This
baseline establishes the overfitting regime that the remaining methods
attempt to improve upon.

\subsection{Weight Decay}
\label{sec:method:wd}
We study decoupled weight decay in AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}
by sweeping
\[
  \lambda \in \{0,\, 10^{-4},\, 10^{-2},\, 5\times 10^{-2},\, 10^{-1}\}.
\]
Weight decay is the only parameter-level regularizer varied in this
study; all other components of the optimization pipeline are fixed to
the shared baseline in \cref{sec:method:baseline,sec:exp:training}. This
design isolates whether shrinking parameter norms during fine-tuning can
improve generalization without confounding the comparison with changes
in augmentation, architecture, or training budget.

The sweep spans three qualitatively different regimes. The first regime,
$\lambda \in \{0,10^{-4}\}$, is effectively a no-decay control and tests
whether a very small penalty is distinguishable from the baseline. The
second regime, $\lambda=10^{-2}$, covers the light regularization range
commonly used with AdamW in modern vision training pipelines. The third
regime, $\lambda \in \{5\times10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$, probes substantially
stronger shrinkage, including a DeiT-style value
\citep{touvron2021deit} and a deliberately aggressive setting intended
to expose the onset of underfitting.

Because the model is initialized from an ImageNet-pretrained checkpoint
and fine-tuned for only 30 epochs, the expected effect size is smaller
than in from-scratch ViT training. The purpose of this sweep is
therefore not only to identify the best coefficient, but also to answer
whether weight decay is a meaningful regularization knob at all in this
short-horizon fine-tuning regime. Concretely, the experiment asks three
questions: whether any $\lambda>0$ improves validation or test
performance over $\lambda=0$, whether the response curve shows a clear
optimum or only a broad plateau, and whether stronger decay reduces
overfitting or instead suppresses effective model capacity.

\subsection{Label Smoothing and Early Stopping}
\label{sec:method:lsearly}
We group label smoothing and early stopping because they regularize at
two distinct stages of the same training pipeline: label smoothing
changes the target distribution seen by the classifier, while early
stopping changes the stopping rule used to select a checkpoint. They
are therefore reported together but analyzed separately in the
experiments.
