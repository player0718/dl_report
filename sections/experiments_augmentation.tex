\subsection{Data Augmentation}
\label{sec:augmentation}


\paragraph{Results.}

The three augmentation settings represent different forms of data-level regularization. RandAugment + Random Erasing applies image-level perturbations, Mixup + CutMix trains on mixed samples and soft labels, and the Combined setting uses both. As summarized in \cref{tab:data_aug}, Mixup + CutMix gives the strongest result among the augmentation methods: it improves the 100-epoch test accuracy from the clean baseline of 79.65\% to 84.97\%, and it reaches 85.72\% test accuracy under the 30-epoch schedule. RandAugment + Random Erasing also improves test accuracy and gives a much smaller measured train--validation gap. The Combined setting remains competitive, but its 100-epoch test accuracy is slightly lower than Mixup + CutMix, suggesting that stacking all augmentations is not necessarily better for this small backbone.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
\label{sec:aug:discussion}

These results are consistent with the fact that ViTs rely less on built-in locality and translation equivariance than CNNs. Data augmentation can partially compensate for this by exposing the model to a wider range of image transformations and label combinations. Mixup and CutMix likely help because they create softer decision boundaries and force the classifier to rely on class-discriminative evidence even when images are partially mixed.

The lower test accuracy and negative measured gap of the Combined setting suggest that this recipe may be too strong for ViT-Tiny. One likely explanation is that stacking several aggressive augmentations removes too much class-relevant visual information from some training samples. For small backbones, combining augmentations may therefore require weaker individual settings, such as lower augmentation magnitude or smaller mixing coefficients, so that the training images remain informative.
