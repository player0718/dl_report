\paragraph{Experiment focus.}
This subsection reports the results of the weight-decay sweep defined in
\cref{sec:method:wd}, evaluated under the shared protocol of
\cref{sec:exp:training,sec:exp:eval}. The only variable that changes is
the AdamW decoupled weight-decay coefficient $\lambda$. Because the group
baseline was later standardized to 100 epochs, we treat the 100-epoch
sweep as the main result and keep the original 30-epoch sweep as a
short-horizon reference.

The table also includes the original 30-epoch validation and test

\paragraph{Train--val gap.}
\Cref{fig:wd-gap} plots the train--val accuracy gap as a function of
training epoch for each $\lambda$. A flat or shrinking gap at large
$\lambda$ is direct evidence that weight decay is constraining
memorization rather than just trading capacity for regularization. In the
100-epoch runs, all settings eventually develop a large gap of roughly
11 percentage points, indicating substantial overfitting under the longer
fine-tuning horizon.

\paragraph{Results summary.}
Under the 100-epoch protocol, moderate weight decay provides a small but
consistent improvement over the no-decay control. The baseline
$\lambda=0$ reaches $81.90\%$ best validation accuracy and $81.81\%$ test
accuracy. The best setting is $\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$, which reaches
$82.56\%$ best validation accuracy and $82.23\%$ test accuracy, improving
test accuracy by $0.42$ percentage points over the no-decay control.
The nearby settings $\lambda=10^{-4}$ and $\lambda=10^{-2}$ also improve
test accuracy by $0.40$ and $0.36$ percentage points, respectively. In
contrast, $\lambda=10^{-1}$ drops to $81.25\%$ test accuracy, suggesting
that overly strong decay begins to suppress useful model capacity.

The 30-epoch sweep gives a different short-horizon picture: the
no-decay control obtains the highest test accuracy ($84.75\%$), while
nonzero decay values remain within a narrow band. This discrepancy
should not be interpreted as a contradiction. Changing the total number
of epochs also changes the cosine learning-rate trajectory
($T_{\max}=E-3$ after the 3-epoch warmup), so the 100-epoch run is not
merely the 30-epoch model trained for 70 additional epochs. Instead, the
longer schedule exposes stronger overfitting, as shown by the train--val
gap increasing from roughly 5 percentage points in the 30-epoch runs to
roughly 11 percentage points in the 100-epoch runs. In this longer
regime, moderate weight decay becomes mildly beneficial but still not a
dominant source of generalization improvement.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:wd:limitations}
These runs use a single seed per configuration, so sub-$0.5$ point
differences should be interpreted cautiously because they may fall
within run-to-run noise. In addition, the study fine-tunes from an
ImageNet-pretrained checkpoint rather than training from scratch, so the
conclusions do not necessarily transfer to the longer, more strongly
regularized training regime often used for DeiT-style recipes
\citep{touvron2021deit}. The small absolute spread across neighboring
$\lambda$ values is therefore itself a result: within this controlled
fine-tuning setup, weight decay is a weak but measurable regularization
knob rather than a dominant factor.
