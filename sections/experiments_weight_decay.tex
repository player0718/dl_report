\label{sec:wd}

\paragraph{Results.}
As shown in \cref{tab:wd}, moderate AdamW weight decay yields a small but
measurable improvement under the 100-epoch protocol, whereas the strongest
coefficient degrades accuracy. The no-decay baseline reaches $81.90\%$
best validation accuracy and $81.81\%$ test accuracy. The best setting,
$\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$, improves these values to $82.56\%$ and
$82.23\%$, respectively, corresponding to gains of $0.66$ pp in validation
accuracy and $0.42$ pp in test accuracy. The moderate settings form a
narrow performance plateau (shaded in \cref{fig:wd-summary}):
$\lambda=10^{-4}$, $10^{-2}$, and $5\times10^{-2}$ differ by only
$0.06$ pp in test accuracy. In contrast, $\lambda=10^{-1}$ drops to
$81.25\%$ test accuracy. In the 30-epoch
reference runs, the best test accuracy is obtained by $\lambda=10^{-2}$
($84.74\%$), while $\lambda=10^{-1}$ gives the highest validation accuracy
($84.56\%$); all 30-epoch gaps remain much smaller than those observed
after 100 epochs.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
These results suggest that weight decay mainly shifts the accuracy level
rather than eliminating the generalization gap. Across the 100-epoch
sweep, the train--validation gap remains within $11.02$--$11.55$ pp
(\cref{fig:wd-summary}, right axis), even though the best test accuracy
changes across $\lambda$. This pattern indicates that weight decay alone
only weakly controls the overfitting observed in the longer schedule. The drop at $\lambda=10^{-1}$ is
consistent with over-regularization: compared with
$\lambda=5\times10^{-2}$, it loses $1.12$ pp in best validation accuracy
and $0.98$ pp in test accuracy while still retaining a large
train--validation gap. The 30-epoch results should therefore be interpreted
as a short-horizon reference rather than a direct contradiction, because
changing the epoch budget also changes the cosine learning-rate trajectory.
Overall, weight decay provides a limited improvement in the longer
fine-tuning regime and motivates comparison with other regularization
mechanisms.
